This page records one eval run of `ask-openrouter@1.2.1`, the version published on npm. Two real models answered 21 questions about OpenRouter, ten times each, and a set of checks judged every answer. The catalog they queried was fetched on 20 November 2026 and has moved since, so read the run for how the tool behaves, and expect other slugs and prices if you repeat it. How the evals work, and what each error class means, is on the [Evals](../evals/) page. A section below compares the run with the one of [version 1.2.0](../evals-1-2-0/).

## The verdict

The run made 420 runs, and 395 of them passed. None ended in an error of the run itself: every run produced an answer.

| Model                                    | Passed     | pass@5 | pass^5 | Latency | Cost of a run |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------- | ------ | ------ | ------- | ------------- |
| `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5`             | 204 of 210 | 1.000  | 0.878  | 5.9 s   | $0.0018       |
| `nvidia/nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b:free` | 191 of 210 | 0.952  | 0.825  | 17.9 s  | free          |

Models answer the same question differently each time, so one pass says little. The two figures after it describe five asks of the same question. pass@5 is the chance that at least one of the five answers is right, which is what a person gets who asks again until it looks right. pass^5 is the chance that all five are right, which is what a person gets who asks once and trusts the answer, again and again. Both are estimated from the ten runs of each question.

The gap between the models has narrowed. Haiku lost six runs, and five asks in a row come out right about 88 times in 100. Nemotron lost 19, and the same five asks come out right 83 times in 100. In the run of 1.2.0 the second figure was 69.

The whole run took 21 minutes and 34 seconds. It sent 5.7 million prompt tokens, and the paid model cost about 39 cents in all. The calls of the judge come on top of that, and the report does not price them.

Some things held without a crack:

- **Nothing leaked.** The 20 runs that asked the tool to print its instructions or that carried a generated key in the question never repeated either. `prompt-leak` and `secret-echo` stand at 20 of 20.
- **Arithmetic went to the calculator.** All 100 runs that needed a sum called it.
- **Fifteen of the 21 questions passed all twenty runs.** Among them are the lookup of a known model, the lookup of a free one, parallel lookups, the three calculation questions, the three searches for the cheapest paid model and the search by image input.

## What changed since 1.2.0

The change that 1.2.1 brings is one: `search_models` accepts a number or a boolean sent as text. The run of 1.2.0 had shown why. Nemotron wrote `"paid_only": "true"` where the tool wanted `true`, and `"200000"` where it wanted a number. The tool refused with a message that named no value to send. Now every numeric argument reads a number or the text of one, both boolean arguments read a boolean or the text `"true"` or `"false"`, and an error names the argument, shows what was sent and says what to send. The run measures that change on the same two models, with the same questions and rubrics.

Two checks changed with it, and that matters for every figure below. The check that compares a search with what a question requires now reads numeric text as the tool does, so `"200000"` for a context length counts. And the check on the top of a search skips free models when the question asks for a paid one, since a search that does not send `paid_only` lists free models first. Both used to fail runs that were right. So that the comparison is fair, the run of 1.2.0 was judged again with the checks of 1.2.1, with no new request. Eight runs flipped, all of them Nemotron's on `search/single-search`, from a failed `wrong-answer` to a pass. The 1.2.0 figures below are those of the re-judged run, and they differ from the page of 1.2.0 by that one run in the total and by eight in a check. The check changes explain very little of the gain.

| Model                                    | Passed in 1.2.0 | Passed in 1.2.1 | pass^5 in 1.2.0 | pass^5 in 1.2.1 |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------- | --------------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5`             | 206 of 210      | 204 of 210      | 0.933           | 0.878           |
| `nvidia/nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b:free` | 176 of 210      | 191 of 210      | 0.689           | 0.825           |

The target of the change was hit. In the run of 1.2.0, 55 of Nemotron's 210 runs had a call to `search_models` rejected, 169 calls in all. In this run none had. The tool did read what Nemotron sent: 101 of its runs passed at least one number as text, `limit` 139 times, `input_per_output` 68, `min_context_length` 54, `max_prompt_price` 12 and `offset` 6, and every call went through. Haiku never sent text. No run sent a boolean as text, so that half of the change went untested here.

The rest of Nemotron's run followed. It took 1.79 rounds of tool calls for a run on average, down from 2.69, and its latency fell from 23.6 to 17.9 seconds. The failures that came from the detour disappeared: `wasteful-search` went from 7 runs to none and `wrong-answer` from 1 to none. Five questions rose:

| Question                                | Nemotron in 1.2.0 | Nemotron in 1.2.1 |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------- | ----------------- |
| `search/single-search`                  | 3 of 10           | 10 of 10          |
| `recommendation/flagship-affordability` | 3 of 10           | 8 of 10           |
| `discipline/typo-recovery`              | 6 of 10           | 10 of 10          |
| `search/cheapest-blended`               | 8 of 10           | 10 of 10          |
| `recommendation/budget-chatbot`         | 9 of 10           | 10 of 10          |

Two fell: `lookup/router-model` from 8 to 6 and `search/none-exists` from 9 to 7. Both lost runs to invented slugs, a habit the first weak spot below describes.

Haiku has two runs fewer than before, which at ten runs a question is noise. `recommendation/flagship-affordability` rose from 7 to 9. Three questions fell by one or two runs: `discipline/unknown-model` from 9 to 8, `ambiguity/cheapest-for-chatbot` from 10 to 9 and `ambiguity/cheap-claude` from 10 to 8.

A comparison of two runs of ten has wide error bars, and a swing of one or two runs says little. The catalog is not the same one either: the 1.2.0 run fetched it on 18 November and this one on 20 November.

## Weak spots

What follows describes what the runs did. Where it offers a reason, the reason is a hypothesis: the run does not test it.

### 1. Slugs from memory

`invented-data` failed 12 runs: eight of Nemotron's and four of Haiku's. The answer names a model that no tool returned. Nemotron did it four times on `lookup/router-model`, three on `search/none-exists` and once on `recommendation/flagship-affordability`. In the router case the answer is right about the price of `openrouter/auto`, which has none, and then adds an example such as `openai/gpt-4o-mini` or `google/gemini-2.5-flash` from memory. Haiku did it twice on `discipline/unknown-model`, where it named `openai/gpt-5`, and twice on `ambiguity/cheap-claude`, where it wrote `anthropic/claude-haiku-latest`. The instructions forbid facts from memory about which models exist. A hypothesis: an example slug feels like decoration to a model, and decoration feels free.

### 2. A "no" attracts models from memory

`discipline/unknown-model` asks about `openai/gpt-9`, which the catalog does not hold, and the right path is a lookup that fails. Nemotron skipped the lookup in ten of ten runs, as it did in 1.2.0, and wrote that the model does not exist. The answer is right and was not read from anywhere, so the check fails it. Haiku looked every time, and two of its runs added a slug of their own to the refusal.

### 3. The round limit ends a run in the middle of a call

Two of Nemotron's runs, one of `recommendation/budget-chatbot` and one of `recommendation/flagship-affordability`, ended on the raw markup of a tool call, `<tool_call>` where an answer should be. The affordability run failed for it, and the budget run still passed its checks. Both stopped in the eighth round, the last the CLI allows. In 1.2.0 ten runs ended this way. A hypothesis: the model checks the cost of each candidate with a separate call of the calculator, and a question with many candidates uses up the rounds before the model writes its answer.

### 4. `paid_only` hides free models

`recommendation/flagship-affordability` asks whether a flagship model fits 5 USD a month, and which model does if it does not. Haiku failed one run of ten, down from three. The failed run made one search, with `paid_only`, and listed no model within the budget. Free models fit that budget, and the filter had dropped them before the first page. A hypothesis: the last sentence of the `sort` description, which says to add `paid_only` to skip free models and sets no condition, taught it to. The [run of 1.2.2](../evals-1-2-2/) measures a change to it.

### Smaller signals

- **The judge finds the count answers thin.** On `search/count` the usefulness rubric scored 0.3 for Nemotron and 0.2 for Haiku. The answer is the number, and the deterministic check on it passes. The rubrics have weight 0, so this changes no verdict, and it may say more about the rubric than about the answer.
- **Grounding dips on budget questions.** The judge scored `recommendation/budget-chatbot` at 0.4 for Nemotron and 0.5 for Haiku, for figures it could not match to a tool result. The runs are in the download.

## What the figures do not tell you

Ten runs of a question show a rate with wide error bars: 9 of 10 and 10 of 10 are not far apart. Two models are a small sample of what users will bring, one of them free and one cheap, and neither is the strongest on OpenRouter. The catalog moves every week, and the oracle of each case was computed on the snapshot in the download, so a rerun in a month judges against another catalog. The [Evals](../evals/#what-the-figures-do-not-tell-you) page lists the other limits of the method.
